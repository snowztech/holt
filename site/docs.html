Holt docs

Holt keeps everything you copy on your Mac. Press ⇧⌘V, type a few letters, and paste it again. Here's everything it does.

Get started

Download Holt, open the disk image and drag Holt to Applications. Or with Homebrew:

brew install --cask snowztech/tap/holt

Holt needs macOS 14 or later on an Apple Silicon Mac.

First launch

A short checklist asks for what Holt needs:

  • See what you copy. Required. Set Holt to Always Allow in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Paste from Other Apps. Without it, macOS asks on every copy.
  • Paste for you. Optional. With Accessibility access, Holt pastes into the app you're in. Without it, Holt puts the clip on your clipboard and you press ⌘V.
  • Open at login. Recommended, so Holt doesn't miss copies after a restart.

Holt lives in the menu bar as Skip's face. It doesn't have a Dock icon.

Updates

Holt checks for new versions by itself. When one is out, the menu bar menu shows Update to Holt. You can also pick Check for Updates in the same menu. With Homebrew, brew upgrade works too.

Find and paste

Press ⇧⌘V anywhere. The popup opens with your newest clips first and the search field ready.

Search

Type any words from the clip, in any order. Every word has to match somewhere, and case doesn't matter, so ssh 10.0 finds that one command from last week. Pinned clips come first, then the newest.

Paste

Move with ↑ ↓ and press ↩. Holt pastes the clip into the app you were typing in. The popup never takes focus away from that app, so the paste lands where your cursor was.

Hold ⌘ to see numbers next to the first nine clips, and press ⌘1 to ⌘9 to paste one directly. Double-clicking a clip pastes it too.

A clip you paste moves back to the top, and keeps the app it was first copied from.

Preview

The right side shows the selected clip in full: where it was copied, when, and on which Mac. Links show as a card. Press ⌘O to open a link in your browser.

Drag out

Drag any clip out of the list and drop it into another app, like a text field, a Mail message or a Finder window.

Delete

⌘⌫ deletes the selected clip, here and on your paired Macs.

Menu bar menu

Click Skip in the menu bar for your 5 most recent clips. Clicking one copies it, so you can paste it with ⌘V. You can also drag a clip out of the menu into any app.

The menu also shows whether your Macs are connected, and has Open Holt, Settings and Check for Updates. If Holt can't read the clipboard, it shows Allow Clipboard Access at the top.

Pins

Press ⌘P on a clip to pin it. Pinned clips stay at the top of the list, never expire, and survive Clear History. Use them for things you paste every day: an address, a signature, a command. Press ⌘P again to unpin.

Images

Screenshots and pictures you copy are kept too, up to 10 MB each, and so are image files you copy in Finder. PNG, JPEG and HEIC images are kept as they are, and other formats are saved as PNG.

Holt reads the text in each image on your Mac, with Apple's text recognizer. Search finds it, so invoice 2026 finds the screenshot of the invoice, and the preview shows the lines that matched. Nothing is sent anywhere.

  • See it full size. Select an image and press space. Press it again or esc to close. In the popup this works while the search field is empty, so you can still type a space in a search.
  • In the menu bar menu. Highlight an image and press space. This needs Accessibility access.
  • Open it. ⌘O opens the image in Preview. Holt's copy is read-only, so it stays as you copied it. Duplicate it in Preview to edit.
  • Paste it. ↩ pastes the image itself, and dragging it out drops the file.

Images sync to your other Macs once they run Holt 0.1.4 or later. To stop keeping images, turn off Record images in Settings → History. Raycast and Alfred don't show images yet.

Sync between Macs

Pair your Macs once. After that, copies, pins and deletes show up on every Mac.

Pair two Macs

  1. Open Holt Settings → Devices on both Macs. They need to be on the same network for this step.
  2. Each Mac shows a 6-digit code and lists the other under Nearby.
  3. On one Mac, click Pair next to the other Mac and type the code the other Mac shows.

Your Macs stay paired from then on, on any network. To stop syncing with one, click Remove next to it in Settings → Devices.

The QR code in that tab is for the iPhone app, which is coming soon.

Copy here, paste there

A clip you copied on another Mac in the last 2 minutes goes straight onto this Mac's clipboard, so you can press ⌘V right away. A small notice says which Mac it came from. You can turn the notice off in Settings → General.

How it connects

  • Same Wi-Fi. Your Macs talk directly over your local network.
  • Different networks. They connect directly over the internet.
  • A router in the way. An encrypted relay passes the data along. It can't read it and doesn't store it.

Sync is end-to-end encrypted and never uses Bluetooth. Only Macs you paired can connect.

Raycast and Alfred

Both read the history Holt keeps on your Mac, so Holt needs to be installed. They use the same search: every word has to match, and pinned clips come first.

Raycast

Run Search Clipboard History from the Holt extension. ↩ pastes the clip, and the action panel can copy it instead or open a link. The extension is waiting for review in the Raycast Store. You can follow it on GitHub.

Alfred

This needs Alfred's Powerpack. Download the Holt workflow and open it to install. Type holt and what you're looking for. ↩ pastes the clip, and ⌘↩ copies it without pasting.

Settings

Open them from the menu bar menu, or press ⌘, while the menu is open.

General

  • Appearance. System, Light or Dark.
  • Open Holt with. Click the shortcut and press the keys you want instead of ⇧⌘V.
  • Open Holt at login.
  • Show clips arriving from other devices. The notice when a copy comes from another Mac.
  • Permissions. Whether Holt can see what you copy and paste for you, with a button to fix each one.

History

  • Keep clips for. 1 week, 1 month, 3 months or 1 year. Older clips are removed, except pinned ones. Holt also keeps at most 5,000 unpinned clips.
  • Record images. On by default.
  • Never record copies from. Add apps whose copies Holt should ignore.
  • Clear History. Removes every unpinned clip, here and on your paired Macs.

Devices

Your paired Macs, whether each is connected, and the code to pair a new one. See Sync between Macs.

Privacy

  • Your history stays on your Macs, in ~/Library/Application Support/Holt. There's no Holt account, no Holt server and no analytics.
  • Copies that password managers like 1Password and Bitwarden mark as private are never recorded.
  • Text in images is read on your Mac and never leaves it.
  • To find each other across networks, your Macs use iroh's discovery and relay servers. Those see your Macs' network addresses, never your clips.

The full privacy policy has the details.

Troubleshooting

Holt doesn't record what I copy

Set Holt to Always Allow in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Paste from Other Apps. While it's missing, the menu bar menu shows Allow Clipboard Access. Also check that the app you copied from isn't in Settings → History → Never record copies from.

Picking a clip copies it but doesn't paste

Turn on Holt in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Settings → General in Holt shows whether it's on.

My other Mac doesn't show up under Nearby

Both Macs need Settings → Devices open and the same network. Also check that Holt is allowed in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network.

The code didn't match

Type the code shown on the other Mac, not this one. After 5 wrong codes that code stops working. Close Settings on the other Mac and open Devices again for a new one.

An image didn't show up

Images over 10 MB are skipped. Also check that Record images is on in Settings → History. If you copied text and an image together, like from a web page, Holt keeps the text.

How do I uninstall Holt?

With Homebrew: brew uninstall --zap --cask holt. Otherwise quit Holt, drag it to the Trash and delete ~/Library/Application Support/Holt.

Something else? Open an issue on GitHub.
